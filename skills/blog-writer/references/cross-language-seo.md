# Cross-language editorial method

Use this method inside W’s research-and-writing loop when the frozen campaign targets another language or region.

1. Inspect current public brand pages for the same locale and intent. Record URL, context and date. This is the highest-priority wording source.
2. When usable, use global Google Trends only with **English seed terms and English long-tail candidates**, regardless of the target language. Record the English source concept/query, `GT seed language = ENGLISH_ONLY`, scope, time window, date and relative signal. It is optional global relative-interest context only: do not compare target-language wording there or infer local search volume, low-base/high-momentum, local demand, commercial intent, or model capability.
3. If Trends is unavailable, insufficient, or inconclusive, do not force repeated collection or manufacture a trend rationale. Choose the long-tail reader problem from current brand wording and target-region SERP semantics. Record the evidence path and an explicit boundary that this is not a popularity or demand claim. Platform articles support reader needs, topic framing, technical depth and delivery choices. They do not replace applicable brand wording or regional SERP evidence for keywords and search intent, and alone do not establish local search demand, volume or popularity.
4. Inspect target-region SERPs to identify reader intent, natural phrasing and modifiers. Record query, date and semantic patterns; never copy competitors.
5. Select a natural, title-ready phrase in strict order: `CURRENT_BRAND_SITE → REGIONAL_SERP → MODEL_TRANSLATION_FALLBACK`. Prefer applicable same-language, same-market brand wording; consult regional usage when suitable brand wording is absent or its fit is uncertain. Do not collect more SERP samples merely to reconfirm an already sufficient wording choice; brand wording alone is not demand evidence.
6. A fallback is permitted only after two independent regional-SERP checks document no usable consensus wording. Record both checks, source concept, proposed wording, language/intent rationale and cultural/legal risk. Never label it SERP-proven.

First inherit any owner-required main phrase and all protected meanings from the article contract; supporting angles must not replace it. Record the selected complete local phrase, rationale and source in the existing `reader_intent_basis`, with the English seed kept only as the source concept. Article title and intended H1 normally preserve all protected meanings in natural, varied sentences; a complete local phrase is mandatory only for explicitly locked wording or an unchanged legacy contract; an English exact-match phrase is not mandatory in a non-English article. Do not treat space-separated query tokens as the final phrase or shorten the phrase merely to pass an existing title. See [editorial-standard.md](editorial-standard.md) for title and H2/H3 checks.

In new lightweight projects, keep the rationale, references and concept-to-local-wording map in the draft's research information; do not add separate research files. Historical indexed workspaces retain their canonical `research/evidence-pack.json` and may derive a reading view, never a second hand-maintained source. If `evidence_posture` is `METHOD_TEMPLATE_NO_EXECUTION`, frame comparisons as reader-run designs rather than results; documented empirical claims require a protocol, settings/inputs, run log, criteria and limitations. Independent R judges reader-task fit, natural wording and factual support in the one final-payload review. Do not add a default pre-draft research review or formal full author self-review. Missing Trends alone is not a defect. G verifies only that the frozen localization requirement is represented in final delivery.
